# LESSONS — falsifiable rule-change hypotheses only

Format: Hypothesis -> Proposed rule change -> Evidence that would confirm/kill

## 2026-08-19 — from TRX full exit, CAKE/ETH/ZEC/MORPHO staged-entry cuts

1. **Named thesis-test breach (TRX).** Hypothesis: when an entry thesis names a single specific confirmation metric (e.g. OI Δ), a sustained reversal in that metric alone — even without 4/10 Bearish or an invalidation breach — should trigger de-risking faster than the general conviction ladder allows. Proposed rule change: add a "named thesis-test breach" trigger — 3 consecutive checkpoints of the entry thesis's stated confirmation metric moving the wrong direction mandates at least a trim, independent of the Bearish-count gate. Evidence to confirm: future positions where this rule fires save realized loss vs. the general gate. Evidence to kill: cases where the named metric round-trips and a fast trim would have cut a position that recovered.

2. **RSI/rvol-only rollover at staged-entry confirmation (CAKE, ZEC).** Hypothesis: staged-entry cuts driven only by RSI and/or realized-vol-ratio cooling from the top of their Bullish bands to Neutral (both mean-reverting, range-bound indicators) while price, OI, and DMA hold or improve are false-negative cuts, not real thesis deterioration. Proposed rule change: at the staged-entry confirmation checkpoint, if the only parameters that rolled over are RSI/rvol while price+OI+DMA all held or improved, hold the half instead of cutting and revisit next checkpoint. Evidence to confirm: held-instead-of-cut positions matching this pattern subsequently re-qualify and outperform a full cut. Evidence to kill: this pattern preceding a subsequent invalidation breach.

3. **Non-golden-cross DMA at entry predicts non-confirmation (ETH).** Hypothesis: an entry whose DMA reads mechanically Neutral (not a clean golden cross) at confluence-gate time is more likely to fail next-checkpoint staged-entry confirmation. Proposed rule change: cap initial stage-1 sizing below the standard half-target for Neutral-DMA entries. Evidence to confirm: a pattern of Neutral-DMA entries failing confirmation at a higher rate than golden-cross entries. Evidence to kill: a Neutral-DMA entry that confirms and performs as well as golden-cross entries.

4. **Overextension at entry predicts fast reversal (MORPHO).** Hypothesis: entries where dev-from-50DMA is the most extended of a confirmed-candidate cohort carry disproportionate same-day reversal risk even when OI backing looks strongest. Proposed rule change: when a confirmed candidate's dev-from-50DMA exceeds ~2x the cohort median at entry, cap its initial stage-1 size below the standard half-target and treat any single-checkpoint OI reversal (not just sustained) as sufficient for a hard cut. Evidence to confirm: future extended entries showing this same fast-reversal pattern. Evidence to kill: extended entries that continue trending without reversal.


5. **Sub-cent price serialisation silently corrupts parameter 2 (SHIB, 2026-08-20 PM).** Hypothesis: `parameters.py` rounds `dma50`/`dma200` to one significant figure for tokens priced below ~$0.00001, so the p2 comparison can evaluate against a moving average that is off by >10% — SHIB's true 50DMA of 4.5016e-06 (price +9.1% above it) serialised as `5e-06`, putting price "below both MAs" and printing a spurious Bearish. Proposed rule change: store and compare DMA values at full float precision (or fixed significant digits scaled to price magnitude) rather than rounded output, and re-derive p2 from `dev_from_50dma_pct` plus an explicit `golden_cross` boolean instead of raw price/MA comparisons. Evidence to confirm: any further micro-priced token (SHIB, PEPE, and any sub-cent rotating add) whose p2 label contradicts its own `dev_from_50dma_pct` sign. Evidence to kill: recomputation from raw klines reproducing the stored labels for all micro-priced tokens across several checkpoints.

7. **Full-cut counterfactual audit quantifies the RSI/rvol false-negative cost, and surfaces a second failure mode the rule doesn't cover (2026-08-21, user-requested audit of the Aug 16-19 wave vs. Aug 21 AM prices).** Six tranches were trimmed/cut/exited between Aug 16-19: TRX trim (-$7.22), TRX exit (-$4.09), CAKE cut (+$11.96), ETH cut (-$0.04), ZEC cut (-$12.86), MORPHO cut (-$18.98) — total realized -$31.23. Holding the same qty at original cost basis to the Aug 21 AM mark instead: TRX +$3.94/+$3.95, CAKE +$60.77 (+12.2%), ETH +$120.22 (+24.1%), ZEC +$185.40 (+18.6%), MORPHO +$32.34 (+6.5%) — total +$406.63. Opportunity cost: **-$437.86** on a $10,000 book, concentrated in a ~72-hour window that happened to precede a market-wide melt-up (F&G 46→72). Two distinct mechanisms are conflated in that number and need to be separated:
   - **(a) Confirms hypothesis #2 and #3 directly, with dollar weight.** CAKE and ZEC (hypothesis #2's RSI/rvol-only rollover pattern) cost $48.81 and $198.26 respectively by being cut instead of held. ETH (hypothesis #3's non-golden-cross-DMA pattern) cost $120.26 — the single largest miss of the six. This is strong, specific evidence that cuts driven by mean-reverting sub-indicators (RSI, rvol) while price/OI/DMA held were false negatives, not real thesis failures, and it happened on the very next occurrence after the pattern was first named.
   - **(b) A distinct, uncovered failure mode: genuine thesis-test breaches also lost money by being cut, purely on regime timing.** TRX's exit (hypothesis #1's exact trigger — a real, repeated, worsening OI divergence) and MORPHO's cut (hypothesis #4's exact trigger — a real same-day OI reversal + overextension) were *correctly identified* as deteriorating by the rules that fired, yet both would still have been profitable to hold, because broad beta overran the idiosyncratic weakness within 48-72h. No parameter-quality fix addresses this — it's a regime-timing problem, not a signal-quality problem.
   Proposed rule change (for (b) only — (a) is already covered by hypotheses #2/#3's existing proposed fixes): when a staged-entry non-confirmation or named-thesis-test-breach trigger fires *and* the regime overlay is broadly improving (F&G rising for 2+ consecutive checkpoints AND >60% of the watchlist green on the day), downgrade a scheduled full cut/exit to a partial (e.g. trim to a quarter-size stub) rather than closing out entirely, so a broad-beta rescue isn't fully missed while position-specific risk is still reduced. This must be weighed explicitly against the standing opposite-tail risk this book has pre-mortemed repeatedly (a sharp reversal, e.g. "BTC -15% overnight") — a stub position sized for the melt-up case is still exposed if the regime flips hard mid-hold, so the rule should not be adopted without testing that failure direction too. Evidence to confirm: future melt-up-regime cuts where the stub outperforms a full cut on a like-for-like basis. Evidence to kill: a melt-up-looking regime reversing hard shortly after a stub is kept, where the stub gives back more than a full cut would have avoided. **N=1 week, single melt-up regime, no reversal case yet observed — not sufficient on its own to change Section 5's rules; flagging as a strong monthly-review candidate (next due 2026-09-01) pending a second observation in either direction.**

8. **Parameter 1 may be structurally one-directional in a rising tape (2026-08-20 PM).** Hypothesis: the p1 rubric's Bullish branch requires *improving* interest without euphoria, or capitulation with an intact thesis — both rare in a melt-up — while its Bearish branch (euphoria / rising interest on a name that has run) fires easily; on 2026-08-20 PM, 28 sentiment reads produced 5 Bearish, 23 Neutral and **0 Bullish** on a day when 27 of 30 coins rose. If p1 can realistically only print Bearish or Neutral while the market rallies, it is not a full tenth of the confluence gate but a one-way brake, and the 7/10 threshold is effectively 7-of-9 during uptrends. Proposed rule change: either widen the p1 Bullish branch to admit "flat interest with a conspicuous absence of euphoria on a rising price" (the XLM/BTC/FIL pattern seen tonight), or formally reclassify p1 as a veto/brake parameter that can subtract but not add, and lower the gate to 7-of-9 accordingly. Evidence to confirm: a multi-month p1 label distribution that stays near-zero Bullish through rising tapes while printing Bearish freely. Evidence to kill: p1 Bullish labels appearing at a normal rate in future rallies (i.e. tonight's zero was a one-day artifact), or the monthly parameter scorecard showing p1's Bearish calls carry enough hit rate to justify its full weight regardless of asymmetry.

9. **Funding/F&G-only rollover at staged-entry confirmation, a beta-driven variant of hypothesis #2 (UNI, 2026-08-22 PM).** Hypothesis: staged-entry cuts driven solely by p6 funding and/or p10 F&G rolling from Bullish to Neutral/Bearish — both regime-overlay parameters that move on broad market conditions rather than idiosyncratic weakness, especially during a melt-up where funding is heating up across most of the watchlist simultaneously — are false-negative cuts when price and OI (the parameters actually named in the entry's own thesis test) hold or improve. UNI's stage-1 half was cut on confluence easing 7→6/10 (p6 funding crossing the ~0.05%/8h crowded-long threshold, p4/p10 also softening to Neutral) even though price rose +1.02% and OI stayed positive (24h +12.5%, 7d +19.3%) between entry and the cut, closing at a small realized gain (+$5.15/+0.08R) rather than a loss. This extends hypothesis #2 (which was specifically about RSI/rvol) to the two global/overlay parameters. Proposed rule change: at the staged-entry confirmation checkpoint, if the only parameters that rolled over are p6 funding and/or p10 F&G while price direction and OI direction both held or improved, hold the half instead of cutting and revisit next checkpoint — mirroring the #2 fix but for the regime-overlay parameters specifically. Evidence to confirm: held-instead-of-cut positions matching this pattern subsequently re-qualify and outperform a full cut, especially during broad melt-up conditions where funding/F&G moves are more likely market-wide than name-specific. Evidence to kill: a funding/F&G-only rollover preceding a subsequent invalidation breach or a genuine reversal in price/OI shortly after.

10. **Full-cut counterfactual keeps widening three days on, ZEC and ETH now the two largest single-ticker misses in the book's history (2026-08-24, Monday shadow-book refresh vs. the 2026-08-21 audit in #7).** The same six Aug 16-19/22 tranches (TRX trim+exit, CAKE/ETH/ZEC/MORPHO staged-cuts, UNI staged-cut), marked against today's prices instead of Aug 21's: total realized unchanged at -$31.23, total virtual-if-held rose from +$406.63 (Aug 21 mark) to **+$1,094.37** (Aug 24 mark) — opportunity cost widened from -$437.86 to **-$1,125.60** over three additional days as F&G climbed 72→73 and the melt-up kept extending. ZEC alone is now +$681.00 virtual vs. -$12.86 realized (a staged-entry cut per hypothesis #2's RSI/rvol pattern), and ETH is +$144.63 virtual vs. -$0.04 realized (per hypothesis #3's non-golden-cross-DMA pattern) — both now dwarf every other single-ticker divergence recorded. This is a second, larger data point for hypotheses #2 and #3 specifically (not just #7's regime-timing point): the false-negative cuts aren't just costing money at the moment of the cut, the cost compounds every checkpoint the melt-up continues, because the rule has no mechanism to reconsider a cut once made. No proposed rule change beyond what #2/#3/#7 already state — this is additional evidence weight, flagged explicitly for the 2026-09-01 monthly review as the single largest quantified line item to date. Evidence to kill any of #2/#3/#7: a reversal that erases some of this virtual gap before the monthly review, which would show the divergence was a temporary artifact of an ongoing melt-up rather than a durable rule flaw.

11. **A Weakening-conviction trim on one position can silently push a different sector over its hard cap (ONDO/AAVE, 2026-08-28 PM).** Hypothesis: trimming a position for thesis reasons shrinks total deployed capital in the denominator of the ≤50%-of-deployed-capital-per-sector rule (Section 3 rule 8) without touching any other sector's numerator — so a same-checkpoint trim in one sector can mechanically breach the cap in an *unrelated* sector that was previously sitting compliant (here, exactly at the 49.99% cap from a prior sector-capped entry). This was caught and corrected in the same checkpoint only because the sector-exposure math happened to be checked as part of writing the report; the rule as currently written has no explicit step requiring a sector-exposure recheck after a trim or exit, only after adds/entries (the existing precedent, AAVE's stage2_add_capped, only guards the add path). Proposed rule change: add an explicit Section 5 step — after any trim, exit, or resize (not just adds) with 2+ positions remaining in any sector, recompute each held sector's %-of-deployed-capital and correct immediately if any sector is now >50%, choosing the correction target by lowest confluence count first (ties broken by whichever position was already most constrained by the same rule at entry). Evidence to confirm: this check catching a real breach again before it compounds past one checkpoint. Evidence to kill: a full quarter of trims/exits with multi-position sectors producing no such breach, suggesting the current ad-hoc check-when-writing-the-report habit is sufficient without a formal step.
    - **Escalation, second and larger occurrence (XLM/ONDO/MORPHO/AAVE, 2026-08-29 AM).** The same mechanism fired again one checkpoint later, at greater scale: an XLM Weakening trim-half plus a full ONDO exit (both unrelated sectors) shrank deployed capital enough that DeFi Lending (MORPHO+AAVE) hit ~74.9% of deployed capital — and this time, fully zeroing the lower-confluence position (AAVE) alone was *not* sufficient to clear the cap (would still land at ~67.3%), requiring a further trim of the higher-confluence, Intact/Bullish position (MORPHO) as well. This is a materially larger and more disruptive correction than the 2026-08-28 precedent (a single half-trim), and it forced a compliance-driven resize of the book's single strongest read that checkpoint (MORPHO, 7/10, 0 Bearish) purely because of unrelated-sector risk actions. Two data points now show this compounds rather than being a one-off: a 2-3 position book with only one multi-position sector is structurally fragile to this cap whenever any other position shrinks or exits. Sharpens the proposed rule change: consider also capping how much a *single checkpoint's combination* of unrelated-sector trims/exits is allowed to force onto a remaining sector's healthiest position — e.g., if a sector-cap correction would require trimming a position graded Intact/Strengthening with 0 Bearish, that is itself a signal the book is over-concentrated in position *count* (too few sectors represented) rather than a signal that position's thesis is impaired, and the monthly review should examine whether a 3-position or smaller book should suspend the 50%-of-deployed-capital rule (while keeping the max-2-positions-per-sector rule) rather than force cascading corrections. Evidence to confirm: a third occurrence forcing an even larger correction, or a case where the forced correction visibly cost more (in realized R) than the risk it was meant to control. Evidence to kill: the book returning to 4-5 positions across 3+ sectors, after which this mechanism naturally stops recurring without a rule change.

12. **`sentiment.py`'s X search can return posts about an unrelated coin sharing the watchlist ticker (CAKE, 2026-08-29 PM).** Hypothesis: for tickers that collide with a more X-active token of the same symbol (here, PancakeSwap's $CAKE vs. an unrelated sub-$1M-mcap Solana memecoin also trading as $CAKE), a plain `$TICKER`/`TICKER` X search can return posts overwhelmingly about the wrong asset (5000%+ 5-minute-candle pump alerts, a ~$618k mcap — two to three orders of magnitude below PancakeSwap's actual mcap), producing a sentiment read with no bearing on the watchlist coin. Caught this checkpoint only by eyeballing the returned `representative_posts` against known facts about the real asset and overriding to Neutral rather than trusting the read — the script itself has no disambiguation signal (no mcap/chain check on the source posts) and would silently mislabel p1 if the mismatch weren't manually noticed. Proposed rule change: either (a) have `sentiment.py` include a coin/chain disambiguator in its search query (e.g. append the project name or contract chain) for tickers with known collision risk, or (b) have the decision engine cross-check `representative_posts` dollar/mcap figures against the coin's actual `PARAMETERS.json` price/mcap before accepting a sentiment read, defaulting to Neutral with a logged data-quality note on mismatch (as done here) rather than skipping the check. Evidence to confirm: a future CAKE (or other collision-prone ticker, e.g. common short symbols) fetch producing another clearly-mismatched read that the manual eyeball check catches. Evidence to kill: several subsequent CAKE fetches returning posts consistent with the real PancakeSwap token, suggesting this was a one-off noisy sample rather than a persistent collision.
